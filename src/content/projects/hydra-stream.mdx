## Problem

A streaming platform needs to decide where data may move as well as how quickly it can move. Kamish encodes placement and transfer policy as explicit broker decisions.

## Architecture

The Rust workspace separates policy, enforcement, append-only storage, ownership, outboxes, routing, and audit records. A gateway, CLI, TypeScript SDK, and frontend expose the system. Earlier research used the name Hydra Stream; this existing route is retained for continuity.

<Diagram steps="Signed policy|Broker enforcement|Audit record" />

## Implementation

The inspected enforcement function evaluates ingress, replication, export, fetch, and promotion operations against class and destination rules. Denials and accepted operations are recorded through the audit layer.

## Engineering decisions

Policy checks precede transfer decisions rather than being inferred from a dashboard. The reviewed PR also distinguishes the local in-memory authority from an external metadata service and verifies trusted policy issuers before installation.

## Contribution

Saksham's merged PR and commit history document gateway, CLI packaging, policy-trust, static-path containment, and release-evidence work. Kamish remains a collaborative project; the portfolio does not attribute the complete platform to one author.

## Limitations

The inspected handoff explicitly does not verify external etcd, KMS, independent witnesses, a production browser deployment, or persistent multi-region rollout. Cryptographic policy checks do not by themselves establish legal compliance, zero data loss, or production readiness.
