## Problem

A segmentation score is only meaningful when the labels, class mapping, and evaluated sample are trustworthy. RoadVision combines pretrained-model inference with tools for investigating those assumptions.

## Architecture

Inference, video processing, corruptions, visualization, and confusion-matrix metrics are separate modules. A Streamlit interface brings image, video, and evaluation workflows together.

<Diagram steps="Scene and labels|Segmentation|Confusion matrix" />

## Implementation

The evaluator filters ignored ground-truth pixels, accumulates class-pair counts, and computes IoU from true-positive, false-positive, and false-negative totals. Its default averages over classes present in predictions or ground truth.

## Engineering decisions

Repository reports distinguish a corrupted label artifact from replacement validation pairs. They also separate CPU inference time from frame-skipped video throughput. These distinctions matter more than a single headline score.

## Results

VERIFIED: the confusion-matrix implementation and explicit ignored-label handling exist. DOCUMENTED: a five-scene audit and corruption results are stored in the repository. They have not been reproduced here and must not be described as full-dataset or real-world driving validation.

## Limitations

The model is pretrained, not a newly established architecture. Five selected scenes do not support broad safety or robustness claims. Invalid predictions are clipped into class range by the evaluator, so upstream validation also matters. No autonomous-driving deployment is asserted.
