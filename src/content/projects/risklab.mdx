## Problem

Risk targeting changes both market exposure and trading costs. RiskLab provides a small, inspectable research setting for studying that interaction.

## Architecture

Python modules separate signals, volatility scaling, backtesting, metrics, tail estimation, and stress testing. A separate C++ Monte Carlo extension is present; it was not loaded or benchmarked in this review.

<Diagram steps="Lagged signals|Risk scaling|Net returns" />

## Implementation

The backtester multiplies supplied signal and volatility exposure, computes turnover from position differences, and deducts basis-point costs. Its contract expects signals and scaling to be shifted before entry.

The inspected accounting computes $r_t^{net}=p_t r_t-c\lvert p_t-p_{t-1}\rvert$, where $c$ is the basis-point cost converted to a fraction. Neutral-date filtering limits how this expression maps to the full trading calendar.

## Engineering decisions

Keeping the accounting function small makes cost arithmetic testable. Causality is a pipeline property: a prefix-stability test of one signal cannot certify all preprocessing and exposure calculations.

## Results

VERIFIED: 13 existing tests passed in an isolated copy on 9 November 2026, covering backtest columns/accounting, signal prefix stability, volatility, and metrics. This is a selected offline subset, not a full research reproduction or C++ speed benchmark.

## Limitations

The backtester removes neutral-signal dates before calculating turnover and wealth. Exit costs and calendar-consistent benchmark comparison therefore need review. The tests that passed do not establish profitable out-of-sample performance.
